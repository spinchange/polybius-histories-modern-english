## 2.45 — The Aetolians Envy the Achaeans

- 2.45 | "and Cleomenes, king of the Lacedaemonians" | UNSURE. This is the ethnonym rule. Βασιλεῖ Λακεδαιμονίων is a royal style, which may count as formal. But the context is narrative, and the same chapter says "the Spartans" four lines later (Λακεδαιμονίους). Editor's call. | "and Cleomenes, king of the Spartans" | LOW

Sense: checked and correct. The ἐλπίσαντες clause, the counterfactual (ἂν ἐπετέλεσαν, εἰ μὴ … παρεῖδον), the οὐ συλλογισάμενοι negation and the οὐχ οἷον … ἀλλὰ τοὐναντίον inversion are all rendered correctly.
Flow: reads cleanly. Title fits.

## 2.62 — The Wealth of Megalopolis

- 2.62 | "most of the bodies, both free and slave, managed to escape to Messene" | σωμάτων means "persons" here. In English, "bodies" reads as corpses, and corpses do not escape. The sentence is garbled for the reader. | "most of the people, both free and slave, managed to escape to Messene" | MED

Numbers checked against the Greek: 6,000 and 2,000 talents. Athens sent 10,000 soldiers (μυρίους; the crib's "twenty thousand" is wrong, and the English is right) and manned 100 triremes. The assessment fell 250 talents short of 6,000. The 300 talents of Megalopolis and the 300 talents of Mantinea are both correct. The negations are correct: οὐχ οἷόν τε, οὐκ εἰκῇ, οὐκ ἀπεοικός, οὐκ ἄν τις … τολμήσειεν, μήτε … μήτε.
Flow: one long sentence (the second paragraph's "For I am not even speaking …") is heavy but grammatical. Otherwise it reads cleanly. Title fits.

## 4.23 — Differences of Opinion Among the Spartans

- 4.23 | Title "Differences of Opinion Among the Spartans" | This title is the 1889 crib's rubric, with the ethnonym swapped. In the chapter, the Spartans speak with one voice through Omias's embassy. The men who divide (διεφέροντο … ταῖς γνώμαις) are the members of Philip's council at Tegea (οἱ μετέχοντες τοῦ συνεδρίου). As it stands, the title misstates the content. | "Philip's council divides over Sparta" | MED

Sense: correct. That covers κατηγορήσοντας … τῶν ἀνῃρημένων ("to denounce those who had been killed"), διὰ τὴν πρὸς αὑτοὺς εὔνοιαν ("loyalty to the Macedonians"), and the μηδενὸς ἐν μηδενὶ … δεύτεροι double negative. The seams are sound. The opening Polyphontas clause came in from 4.22, and the unfinished ἕτεροι δὲ τῶν πρεσβυτέρων sentence opens 4.24.
Flow: reads cleanly.

## 4.48 — Achaeus and Prusias I. of Bithynia

- 4.48 | "treacherously murdered by Apaturius the Gaul" | Τοῦ Γαλάτου. Under STYLE_SHEET (Gauls/Galatians), peoples in Asia Minor are "Galatians". The murder happens beyond the Taurus, in Seleucus III's army in Asia Minor. "Gaul" looks like a carry-over from the crib. | "treacherously murdered by Apaturius the Galatian" | MED
- 4.48 | Title "Achaeus and Prusias I. of Bithynia" | This is the crib's rubric word for word, and STYLE_SHEET says titles do not follow the crib. It also has a stray period in "I.". Prusias appears only in passing. The chapter is about Byzantium's appeals and Achaeus' rise and usurpation. | "Achaeus rises to power beyond the Taurus" | MED

Sense: correct. The genealogy is right: Seleucus II is the father, Seleucus III the eldest son, and Antiochus the younger son. The "about two years" is right. In τοῦτο μὲν οὐ προείλετο … τηρῶν δὲ τὴν βασιλείαν, the negation and the contrast are both kept. The seam from 4.47 (the envoys to Prusias) is sound.
Flow: reads cleanly.
