## 15.14 — Victory of Scipio and Flight of Hannibal

- 15.14 | "this obstacle created a serious difficulty for the Roman commander during the rout" | τὸ τῆς τροπῆς ἐμπόδιον means the obstruction *left by* the rout (the litter of the routed lines), not a difficulty arising "during the rout". | "the obstruction left by the rout created a serious difficulty for the Roman commander." | MED
- 15.14 | "and stationed them directly in front of the enemy center" | αὐτοῦ πρὸ τῆς μάχης: he halted them *where they were*, in front of the ground where the fighting had been, opposite the enemy center. The English leaves out "where they were, in front of the battlefield". | "and stationed them where they were, in front of the battlefield, opposite the enemy center." | LOW
- 15.14 | Title "Victory of Scipio and Flight of Hannibal" | Hannibal's flight is not in this chapter (it comes in 15.15). The title is the 1889 rubric. | "Final struggle at Zama and the Roman victory" | LOW
- Seam: the opening fragment εὐρυχωρίας is already handled at the end of 15.13 ("the open spaces there"). OK.
- Numbers (over 1,500 Romans, over 20,000 Carthaginians, prisoners not many fewer) match the Greek.
- Flow: reads cleanly; the narrative sequence is coherent.

## 39.8 — Polybius Recapitulates His Whole Plan

- 39.8 | "Polybius says near the end of his work that after we had brought these matters to a conclusion, we returned" | The Greek introduces a direct quotation (ὅτι ὁ Πολύβιός φησι: …). Reported "that" followed by "we" mixes persons and garbles the sentence. | "Near the end of his work Polybius says: “Having accomplished these things, we returned from Rome …”" (or drop the frame and use a colon) | MED
- 39.8 | "as though we had already completed the chief points of what went before, and were worthy of the gratitude due to Roman goodwill" | κεφάλαιά τινα τῶν προπεπολιτευμένων means the crowning acts of our *previous political career*. χάριν ἀξίαν τῆς πρὸς Ῥωμαίους εὐνοίας puts these acts in apposition as a fitting return for the goodwill toward the Romans. It does not say "we were worthy of gratitude". Whose goodwill (his toward Rome, or Rome's) is grammatically ambiguous, so keep it open. | "as though we had achieved a kind of crown to our earlier political career, a fitting return for the goodwill between us and the Romans" | MED
- Numbers: the 139th Olympiad (ἐνάτη καὶ τριακοστὴ πρὸς ταῖς ἑκατόν) is correct. Names are correct: Philip son of Demetrius, Cleomenes the Spartan, Ptolemy Philopator. The source file's heading and crib ("Diaeus Becomes Strategus") are the wrong content and were correctly ignored.
- Title fits.
- Flow: the opening sentence is garbled (see above). The rest is clean.
