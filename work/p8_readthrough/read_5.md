## 10.34 — The Submission of the Edetani to Scipio

- 10.34 | "As soon as the capture of Carthage had occurred" | Greek Καρχηδόνος ἅλωσιν means the fall of New Carthage, which Scipio took in 209 BC. Carthage itself was not taken until 146 BC. Under STYLE_SHEET (Places) the city is "New Carthage". As written, a reader could take it for Carthage. | "As soon as the capture of New Carthage had occurred" | HIGH
- 10.34 | "first attached the Iberians to his friendship and loyalty" | αὑτῶν is plural ("their own", meaning the Romans'), not Publius' personal friendship. The difference is small. | "first attached the Iberians to Roman friendship and loyalty" | LOW

Flow: reads cleanly. Title fits: the chapter is Edeco's approach to Scipio, and the next chapter covers the general defection.

## 21.18 — Eumenes Persuaded to Speak

- 21.18 | "since he knew Asian affairs and his own interests more exactly than anyone else" | Greek εἰδέναι τὰ διαφέροντα τοῖς ἰδίοις πράγμασιν ἐκεῖνον ἀκριβέστερον τὰ κατὰ τὴν Ἀσίαν means "he knew better what in Asian affairs bore on his own interests". There is one object, not two coordinated ones. The sense shift is slight. | "since he knew more exactly than they what in the affairs of Asia concerned his own interests" | LOW

Flow: clean. The chapter-final sentence ("After this decision he was called back in ... compelled to speak on the matter") is present. A note outside my scope: 21.19 opens with "So he was forced to address the question", which repeats the end of 21.18. This may be a seam duplicate for whoever has 21.19. Title fits.

## 22.16 — Philip's Clemency and Ptolemy's Cruelty

- 22.16 | "instead they compete in anger and punishments against those with whom they are at war, for that very reason." | Greek ἁμιλλῶνται ... πρὸς τούτους οἷς πολεμοῦσι τούτων αὐτῶν ἕνεκα is ambiguous. It may mean "against those they fight, over the very things they are fighting about". It may also mean "for the sake of these very things", i.e. anger and punishment as ends in themselves. The English "for that very reason" has no clear antecedent and reads as garbled. UNSURE which reading is right, so the fix keeps the ambiguity. | "instead they vie in anger and punishment against those they are at war with, on account of those very things." | LOW (UNSURE)

Otherwise the fragmentary opening keeps every papyrus gap as `[...]`, and nothing is supplied across a gap. "Philip" is Philip II (Chaeronea), as in the Greek, and needs no change. Ptolemy (V) and the cart punishment match 22.17. The source file's heading ("Another Commission For Greece") is the wrong crib rubric. The English title is correct and fits the content.

Flow: the opening paragraph is necessarily broken. The rest is clean apart from the sentence flagged above.

## 25.4 — The Senate Hears the Lycian Envoys

- 25.4 | "For the Romans seemed to be acting as umpires between the Rhodians and the Lycians because they wanted the Rhodians' accumulated resources and treasuries to be drained away" | διαγωνοθετεῖν here means staging a contest between, or setting them against each other. It does not mean refereeing. "Umpires" removes the point of the sentence: the Romans were stirring up the quarrel so that Rhodes would exhaust its funds. | "For the Romans seemed to be pitting the Rhodians and the Lycians against each other because they wanted the Rhodians' accumulated resources and treasuries to be drained away" | MED

Names and numbers check: Tiberius (Sempronius Gracchus) and Claudius were consuls of 177 BC. Nicostratus, Xanthus, the ten commissioners and Laodice all match the Greek. ἀφρακτιτῶν is correctly rendered "undecked ships". Title fits: envoys first, then the Rhodian and Perseus background.

Flow: clean.

## 38.19 — Scipio Rejects Caltrops at Carthage

- 38.19 | "scatter iron caltrops or drive in sharpened stakes" | σανίδας ἐμβαλεῖν κεντρωτάς means "throw in spiked planks", boards studded with points. It does not mean stakes. | "scatter iron caltrops or throw in spiked planks" | MED

Flow: one long sentence, grammatical and clear. "He" is Scipio Aemilianus, who is unnamed in the fragment but named in the title. Title fits.
