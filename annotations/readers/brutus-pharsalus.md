During the campaign all the daytime when he was not with Pompeius he was
employed about study and books; and not only at other times, but also
before the great battle. It was the height of summer, and the heat was
excessive, as they were encamped close to marshy ground; and those who
carried the tent of Brutus did not come quickly. After being much
harassed about these matters, and having scarcely by midday anointed
himself and taken a little to eat, while the rest were either sleeping
or engaged in thought and care about the future, he kept on writing till
evening-time, making an epitome of Polybius.

**Note:** On the eve of Pharsalus in 48 BC, with the Republic's fate about to be settled, the man who would help kill Caesar four years later spent a sweltering afternoon abridging Polybius. Plutarch tells it as a portrait of the scholar-soldier, but beside this chapter it reads as something sharper: a Roman in the middle of a civil war, reaching for the book that explained how Rome came to rule the world, and taking literally Polybius' promise that history teaches us to bear changes of fortune. The epitome itself is lost.
