The Roman ambassadors met him after crossing the river at Eleusine, four miles from that city. On their approach he saluted them, and held out his right hand to Popilius; but Popilius put into his hand a written tablet, containing the decree of the senate, and desired him first to peruse that. On perusing it, he said, that he, after calling his friends together, would consult on what was to be done; on which Popilius, with the usual asperity of his disposition, drew a line round the king, with a wand which he held in his hand, and said, "Before you go out of that circle, give me an answer to report to the senate." Astonished at such a peremptory injunction, the king hesitated for some time; but at last replied, "I will do as the senate directs." Popilius then thought proper to stretch out his right hand to him; as to a friend and ally.

**Note:** Livy tells the scene in the same order as Polybius (29.27): the offered hand, the tablet read first, the request to consult friends, the circle drawn with a rod, the surrender, the handshake. He gives it a place (Eleusine, four miles from Alexandria), Antiochus's actual words, and the order Popilius speaks, which Polybius reports only indirectly. Polybius, for his part, stops to guess why Popilius withheld the handshake (he wanted to know first whether the man was friend or enemy) and has Popilius's whole party greet the king, where Livy has Popilius alone offer his hand.
