Scipio, beholding this spectacle, is said to have shed tears and publicly lamented the fortune of the enemy. After meditating by himself a long time and reflecting on the rise and fall of cities, nations, and empires, as well as of individuals, ... Polybius wrote this down just as he heard it.

**Note:** The fullest account of this scene is not Polybius' own but Appian's, writing some three centuries later. Editors print it among the fragments as 38.22 because it names Polybius as its source: he "wrote this down just as he heard it." What survives in Polybius' own words is the broken 38.21, where the Homeric line itself is lost but the witness speaks in the first person: Scipio, "turning at once and grasping my right hand," fears that the same order will one day be given about Rome. Whether the roll of fallen empires (Troy, Assyria, Media, Persia, Macedon) was Polybius' or Appian's, we cannot tell.
