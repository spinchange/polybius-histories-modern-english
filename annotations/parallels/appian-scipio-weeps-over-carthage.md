Scipio, beholding this spectacle, is said to have shed tears and publicly lamented the fortune of the enemy. After meditating by himself a long time and reflecting on the rise and fall of cities, nations, and empires, as well as of individuals, ... Polybius wrote this down just as he heard it.

**Note:** Appian, writing some three centuries later, tells the scene that survives only in tatters in Polybius (38.21-22): Scipio weeping over the burning city, the Homeric line about the fall of Troy, the fear that Rome's turn would come. He adds a roll of fallen empires (Troy, Assyria, Media, Persia, Macedon) and closes by naming his source: Polybius "wrote this down just as he heard it." Polybius' own broken fragment confirms it in the first person, with Scipio "turning at once and grasping my right hand."
