# Book 29 Chapter 1: Speech of L. Aemilius Paullus

Olympiad 152, year 4. Affairs in Italy.

For he said that they had only one occupation, both at dinner parties and in their conversations while walking about: seated in Rome, they managed the war in Macedonia, now criticizing what the generals were doing, now going through what they had left undone.

From this, he said, no benefit ever came to public affairs, but much harm had often resulted in many cases; and at times the commanders themselves were seriously damaged by these ill-timed displays of clever talk.

For slander always has something sharp and stirring about it, and when the mass of people has been prejudiced by constant chatter, the generals become easy for their enemies to despise.
