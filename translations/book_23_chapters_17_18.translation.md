# Book 23 Chapter 17: Arguments For and Against Admitting Sparta

The Messenians, by their own ignorance, had brought themselves to the very edge of ruin, but through the magnanimity of Lycortas and the Achaeans they were restored to their original position in the federal union.

At this same time Abia, Thuria, and Pharae separated themselves from Messene and, after each set up its own inscribed stele, joined the common federation independently.

When the Romans learned that affairs at Messene had turned out in the proper way for the Achaeans, they ignored their earlier reply and gave a different answer to the same envoys, making it clear that they had taken measures to prevent anyone from Italy from bringing either arms or grain into Messene.

From this it became obvious to everyone that they were very far from neglecting outside affairs unless absolutely necessary; quite the opposite, they were annoyed whenever everything was not referred to them and carried out according to their judgment.

When the envoys from Rome arrived in Sparta bringing this answer, the Achaean general, as soon as he had finished settling affairs at Messene, summoned the people to the city of the Sicyonians.

Once the Achaeans had assembled, he laid before them a proposal to admit Sparta into the federation,

saying that the Romans had declined the arbitration previously entrusted to them concerning that city, for they had now answered that affairs at Sparta were no concern of theirs,

while those who at present controlled Sparta wished to take part in the federation.

Therefore, he urged them to admit the city, for this would be advantageous in two ways. First, they would be receiving men who had preserved their loyalty to the League.

Second, they would not have as partners in the constitution those old exiles who had behaved ungratefully and impiously toward them; rather, by confirming the policy of those who had excluded such men from the city, they would both uphold the decision already taken and pay them the gratitude they deserved, with the favor of the gods.

With these and similar arguments Lycortas urged the Achaeans to receive Sparta.

But Diophanes and certain others tried to support the exiles, urging the Achaeans not to join in crushing men who had been banished, and not, because of a handful of people, to strengthen those who had impiously and unlawfully driven them from their fatherland.

# Book 23 Chapter 18: Sparta Included In the League

Such were the arguments made by both sides. After hearing them both, the Achaeans decided to admit the city; and after an inscribed stele had been drawn up, Sparta was politically united with the Achaeans,

the men then in possession of the city agreeing to receive such of the old exiles as were not thought to have committed any outrageous act against the Achaean nation.

After ratifying these measures, the Achaeans sent envoys to Rome, headed by Bippus of Argos, to explain everything to the senate.

The Spartans likewise appointed envoys led by Chaeron.

And the exiles sent Cletis and Diactorius to appear in the senate in opposition to the envoys from the Achaeans.
