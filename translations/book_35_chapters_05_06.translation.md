# Book 35 Chapter 6: Cato's Wit

When Polybius, through Scipio, had been granted an audience on behalf of the exiles from Achaia, the Senate debated the matter at length, with some supporting their return and others opposing it.

Then Cato stood up and said: "As though we had nothing else to do, we spend the whole day discussing whether old Greek men should be carried out by undertakers from our own country or from Achaia.

After the vote had been passed allowing the men to return, Polybius and his friends, after a few days, tried again to enter the Senate so that the exiles might recover the honours they had held in Achaia before, and they tested Cato's view on the matter.

He smiled and said that Polybius, like another Odysseus, wanted to go back into the Cyclops's cave a second time, because he had forgotten his cap and belt there."
