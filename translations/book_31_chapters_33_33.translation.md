# Book 31 Chapter 33: Chapter 33

When Menochares arrived at Antioch to see Demetrius and gave the king a full account of the meeting he had had with Tiberius and his party in Cappadocia,

Demetrius decided that, of all the business before him, the most necessary was to cultivate those men as far as he could.

So he treated everything else as secondary and kept sending messages to them, first to Pamphylia and then again to Rhodes, undertaking to do whatever the Romans required, until he succeeded in being acknowledged by them as king.

For Tiberius was especially well disposed toward him, and so he contributed greatly to Demetrius's success in establishing himself and securing the royal power.

And Demetrius, seizing the opportunity just mentioned, at once sent envoys to Rome, bringing a crown, the man who had with his own hand killed Gnaeus, and along with them Isocrates the literary critic.
