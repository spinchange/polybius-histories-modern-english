# Style Sheet — Polybius, *The Histories*

Status: FINAL (Phase 7). Rules marked **DECIDED** were fixed by the editor (2026-10-31; proposals adopted 2026-11-01).

## Names and peoples
- **Gauls / Galatians — DECIDED.** Peoples in Italy and Europe are "Gauls" ("Gallic"); peoples in Asia Minor are "Galatians". "Celts" appears only where Polybius makes an explicitly ethnographic point (the Book 2 excursus may keep one framing mention).
- **Spartans / Lacedaemonians — DECIDED.** "Spartans" in narrative. "Lacedaemonians" only in formal or diplomatic contexts (treaties, embassies, official self-description).
- **Places.** Rivers take modern names (Ebro, Po, Danube, Rhone — DECIDED). Cities and regions keep ancient names (New Carthage, Iberia).
- **Personal names** use the usual English forms of the Latinized or Hellenized name (Hannibal, Scipio, Philip). Roman *nomina* are written in full only where Polybius does so or the sense requires it.

## Dates and numbers — DECIDED
- Years: `215 BC` (no periods, BC after the number). Ranges take an en dash: `210–206 BC`. Headings follow the same rule.
- Numbers of troops, ships and money are given as Polybius gives them; no silent rounding or correction. Where a figure looks corrupt, that is a note, not an edit.

## Units
- Distances: **stades** throughout. The camp and march chapters of Book 6 give measurements in **feet**, as the Greek does (plethra and feet); these are kept, not converted.
- Money: **talents** and **drachmas** as in the Greek; Roman sums in **denarii** or **asses** only where the Greek uses them.
- No modern-unit equivalents in the running text.

## Offices and institutions
- **Senate** (capital) for the Roman Senate; "senate" (lower case) for other cities' councils, and in the generic sense.
- **consul, praetor, tribune, dictator, censor** are Roman offices, lower case in running text, capital before a name (Consul Gaius Flaminius is avoided; use "the consul Gaius Flaminius").
- **strategos** is rendered by context: "general" for a field commander; "strategus" (italic not required) only for the Achaean, Aetolian and other league offices, where it is an office with a fixed term.
- Greek and Latin technical terms are kept in English dress and explained once, in context, not in footnotes.

## Titles and headings
- Chapter headings: `## Book N Chapter M: Title`. Titles are in **title case**: first and last words and all major words capitalised; articles, short prepositions and conjunctions (a, an, and, as, at, but, by, for, from, in, into, of, on, or, over, the, to, with, and similar) lowercase. Amended 2026-11-01 from the original "sentence case" draft to match the corpus (1,310 headings); `work/scripts/heading_report.py` enforces it.
- Titles describe the chapter's content; they do not follow the 1889 crib's rubrics.

## Gaps, fragments and attribution
- **One lacuna marker: `[...]`.** It stands for text the Greek lacks or breaks off in. Nothing is supplied across a lacuna.
- Chapter breaks that fall mid-sentence were repaired by moving the unfinished sentence under the next heading, not by marking it.
- Fragments preserved through later authors (Books 6 and 7 onward) carry an attribution line where the source names the quoting author (Strabo, Suidas, Stephanus, Athenaeus, Plutarch). Where it does not, the chapter is left unattributed rather than guessed.
- Book 17 is a survival note only ("Nothing of this book survives"). Book 40 is not included.

## Quotation and punctuation
- American spelling; double quotation marks for speech and for quoted documents; single marks only inside doubles.
- Em dashes without spaces. En dashes for ranges.
- Italics for titles of works and for editorial notes inside the text.
